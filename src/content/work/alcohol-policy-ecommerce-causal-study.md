## The question

When a government tightens penalties for drinking and driving, demand doesn't simply disappear — it may move. **Did the policy change sales of alcoholic beverages on e-commerce platforms, and by how much, compared with what would have happened anyway?**

## What I did

- Framed the policy as a natural experiment and used **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)** to separate the policy effect from market-wide trends.
- Compared affected and unaffected segments of the e-commerce market before and after the policy, so the estimate reflects the policy rather than general market growth.

## Why it matters for business analytics

Most business questions are causal questions in disguise: *did the campaign work, did the price change hurt volume, did the new policy shift demand?* Correlation in a dashboard can't answer them. DiD, holdouts and careful counterfactuals can — and that's the toolkit I bring to commercial analysis.
